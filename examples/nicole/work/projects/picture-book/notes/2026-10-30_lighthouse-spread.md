# Lighthouse spread

## What I did

Spread nine of [[Picture Book]] is the turn of the story. The keeper is stuck across the bay, the storm is in, and Mop climbs the stairs to the lamp room alone. The text is two lines, so the picture has to carry the fear and the bravery.

On the evening of the 29th I drew three thumbnails at the size of a matchbox. Version A had the lighthouse from the outside, small, in the middle of a stormy sea. It was dramatic, but Mop was a dot, and the reader would lose the cat in the moment the book is about her. Version B looked up the spiral stairs from below, with Mop at the top. I loved it that night. Version C was inside the lamp room, Mop a dark shape against the big lens, with the beam leaving the window and going out across the water.

I slept on them. This morning B looked busy and a little frightening for a four-year-old, all those stairs. C was calm and clear, so I chose C.

I worked it up at full spread size in pencil. The lamp and Mop go on the left page, well away from the gutter, because anything in the middle of a spread disappears into the binding. The beam crosses the gutter on purpose and lands on the right page, on the keeper's small boat, so the eye travels from the cat to the person she is keeping the light on for. I kept the top right of the sky empty for the two lines of text.

## What I learned

The thumbnail I love on the night I draw it is not always the right one. In the morning I could see that B was about my pleasure in the perspective, not about a child turning the page.

Turning the beam into the line that leads the eye across the spread solved two problems at once, the gutter and the story. In the other spreads I want to look for one shape that sets the reading order.

## What is open

- How to paint the beam. Masking fluid gives a hard clean edge but can tear damp paper; lifting it out with a damp brush is softer but less bright. I will test both on scraps of the same paper.
- The sky. It has to be stormy but not black, and it has to sit behind the text. I am trying grey washes with a little warm yellow under them.
- Whether Mop needs a highlight on her eye so she does not read as just a hole in the lens.

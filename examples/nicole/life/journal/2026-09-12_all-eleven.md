# All Eleven

Eleven pieces in, eleven out. I keep writing the number because I do not quite believe it yet.

I have not told anyone this, not even Joe. On Monday night I looked up what a second-hand drawing desk costs and thought about giving up my half of the kiln. Three loads in August, three loads with something split down the middle, and each time I opened the lid I felt my face go hot, as if Ines were watching me fail an exam. She never said one unkind thing. That almost made it worse. I wanted her to be annoyed so I could be annoyed back.

Instead she put her hand on the shelf of greenware on Tuesday, said they were still cold, and told me two more days. I argued. I said the forecast was dry, I said they looked white, I said the kiln slot was booked. She just held one bowl to her cheek and then held it to mine. It was cold. I had never once thought to check that way. I went home and sulked and ate a whole bag of the bakery's broken biscuits.

This morning I lifted the lid and nothing had cracked. Not the thin bowls, not the mug with the handle I was sure would pop. I held the first bowl and my hands were shaking, and I laughed in a way that made Ines laugh too, and then I had to go and stand outside by the bins for a minute because I was about to cry over a cereal bowl.

What I feel tonight is mostly relief, and under it something quieter. I had decided the cracks meant I was bad at this. They did not. They meant I was in a hurry. Those are very different things, and only one of them is fixable.

I am keeping the half of the kiln. I am keeping the desk under the north window. I am going to buy Ines the good coffee.

On the kiln log this is one line, [[First Firing]], a date and a count. For me it was the week I nearly quit.

# Rain on the Roof

It rained on the tin roof of the [[Kiln Studio]] from lunch until dark, the loud kind of rain where you stop hearing your own thoughts and then, a bit later, hear nothing but them. Ines went home at two. I stayed to paint sky washes for the lighthouse spread, because yesterday's thumbnail finally felt right and I wanted to catch it before I talked myself out of it.

The paper would not dry. Everything in the room was damp, the sheets were damp, my cuffs were damp. Every second wash bloomed. The water crept back into the half-dry pigment and left those cauliflower edges that look pretty on a postcard and wrong on a page for four-year-olds. I ruined five sheets before I admitted I was doing it again. I laid a wash, poked at it, and laid another one on top, because waiting felt like losing the afternoon.

I want to write down what I actually felt, because I keep not saying it. I am scared that sixteen spreads is more than I can do. One spread I can carry in my head. Sixteen is a whole book with my name on the cover, and every cracked pot and every bloomed sky this autumn reads to me like a vote on whether I am allowed to be doing this. That is a stupid way to count. I know it is stupid. It still sat in my chest all afternoon like a wet sponge.

At about five I put the brush in the jar and made tea and did nothing. I watched kids in bin-bag witch capes run past the north window toward the bakery, which gives out burnt gingerbread on Halloween. When I went back, the last wash had dried flat and even, the exact grey-gold I wanted over the water. I had not done anything to it. I had only left it alone.

I thought about the morning in September when Ines would not let me fire, how angry I was for two days, and how it all came out whole. I have had to learn this again with a brush in my hand, and I suspect I will have to learn it again after that. I wrote [[Let It Dry]] on the back of the good sheet in pencil, small, so nobody sees it but me.

Walked home with the hood up. The harbor smelled of diesel and seaweed. I felt better and a little embarrassed about how bad I had felt.

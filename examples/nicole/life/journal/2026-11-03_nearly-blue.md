# Nearly Blue

The kiln was cool enough to open at ten. Ines let me do it, which she does when she knows I am nervous, and pretended to sort her shelf while I lifted the tiles out one at a time.

The middle tile is nearly it. Where the glaze pooled in the carved line it is the blue of the water under the ferry ramp on a still morning, that deep blue with grey in it. I stood there holding it to the window and my heart actually jumped. Then I turned it, and on the raised edges it breaks to the same tired grey-green as every round before.

I did not expect "almost" to feel worse than "wrong". A grey tile I can shrug off. This one showed me the colour is in there, that I am close, and that I still do not know how to get the rest of the way. I was short with Ines at lunch about nothing, about the radio, and I apologised later and she said she had done the same thing over a celadon for a whole winter once.

I wanted to change everything at once for the next round. More cobalt, a different base, thicker application, all of it, today. I made myself write one change on the tile instead. Same recipe, slow cool. I hate that it is the right call.

Told Joe about it on the walk home. He asked what the blue looked like and I tried to describe the ferry ramp and ended up laughing because I was so cross about a square of clay the size of a biscuit. That helped. He always asks what it looks like, never whether it worked.

Back to [[The Blue Glaze]] next week. I put the nearly blue tile on the windowsill above my desk so I see it every morning. I wrote something like this in September, the night of [[All Eleven]]: the cracks meant hurry, not failure. Grey-green means one more round. It does not mean I cannot do this.

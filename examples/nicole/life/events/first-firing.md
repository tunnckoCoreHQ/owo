# First Firing

## What happened

In August I rented kiln time at the [[Kiln Studio]] before I had moved my desk in, and three bisque loads in a row came out cracked. Bowl floors split, a mug handle popped clean off, and one plate cracked from rim to centre. I blamed the kiln, then the clay, then myself.

The fourth load was eleven pieces, mostly thin cereal bowls and two mugs. They looked bone dry to me on the Tuesday and I wanted to fire that night. [[Ines]] touched them, held one bowl against her cheek and then against mine, and said they were still cold, which means still wet inside. She made me wait two more days. I was cross about it the whole time.

We fired on the Thursday with a slow first segment that Ines programmed, holding the kiln under boiling point for the first few hours. I opened the lid on Saturday morning, 12 September, and all eleven came out whole.

## Key facts

- It was my fourth bisque load at the studio and the first with nothing cracked. Eleven pieces went in and eleven came out.
- The fix was two extra drying days and a slow start to the firing. I changed nothing else.
- The cheek test, a pot held to the cheek to feel if it is still cold, is how I check greenware now.
- It was the week I almost gave up my share of the kiln. After it I kept the share and moved my desk in for good.

## Lines

- [[Ines]], on the Tuesday, with the bowl against my cheek: "Clay tells you when it is ready."
- Ines on Saturday, when I could not stop counting them: "Eleven. Write that down somewhere."

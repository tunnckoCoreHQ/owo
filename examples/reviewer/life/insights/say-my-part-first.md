# Say My Part First

When something goes wrong and part of it is mine, I say my part first, out loud, before anyone has to ask for it. I used to think I did this already. The rollback showed me I only did it when it was easy.

During [[The Rollback]], [[Mina Okafor]] asked in the incident channel whether it was her fault. Her name was on the commit. Mine was on the approval. For a few seconds before I answered, part of me was glad the commit said Mina. I answered "it's ours" fast, so that part would not get a vote. But I noticed it, and I have not stopped noticing it since.

I had always pictured blame as something other people put on me. That day it was something I could quietly let slide onto someone else, and nobody would have said a word. She was newer, the code was hers, and I am the senior one people believe. Silence would have been enough.

Two days later I opened the [[Rollback postmortem]] by saying I approved the change and missed the one question that mattered. The room changed after that. Mina stopped defending her migration and started explaining it. Teo said he had shipped the same kind of rename on the cache last year and got lucky. Once the most senior person in the room has said "mine", nobody else needs to protect themselves first.

What it changed in me: I say my part early, while it still costs something, and not once the facts have made it safe. I watch for that small relief when blame lands somewhere else, because it is the signal that I am about to stay quiet. And I try to show Mina that owning a mistake is a normal thing a person does on an ordinary Monday morning, not a speech.

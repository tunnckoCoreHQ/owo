# My Name on It

It is nearly midnight and I am still at the kitchen table with the notebook open. I keep writing the same sentence in it: "what runs during the deploy?" Fifteen years, and I could have asked that in my first year.

The worst moment was not the page, and not the graph of failed transfers going vertical. It was Mina typing "Is it mine?" in the channel, and the three seconds before I answered. In those three seconds some small part of me was relieved that her name was on the commit. I am not proud of that. I answered fast so that part would not get a vote. "It's ours. I approved it." That is true, and I am glad I said it. I am less glad that I had to choose to.

She is good. She writes careful code and she asks me to be direct with her, and on Thursday I approved her rename in about four minutes because it looked like a rename. I gave her code the review and her migration a glance. If she learns from today that review is a safety net, I have taught her the wrong thing. I need to show her what I missed and how, without turning it into a lecture about my own guilt.

What I feel, honestly: embarrassed, more than anything. Calm on the call, shaky after. A little angry at the Saturday deploy, which is not the point and I know it. And under all of that, a kind of relief that it failed loudly. The quiet failures are the ones that cost money.

Tomorrow I will not write about this. On Monday I will lead the postmortem and say my part first.
